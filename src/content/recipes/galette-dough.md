## Ingredients

- ½ cup (115 g) water
- 1 tablespoon apple cider vinegar
- 2¾ cups (345 g) all-purpose flour, spooned and leveled, plus more as needed
- 1 tablespoon sugar
- 1½ teaspoons Diamond Crystal or ¾ teaspoon Morton kosher salt
- 2½ sticks (10 ounces/285 g) unsalted butter, cut into ½-inch (13 mm) cubes and chilled

## Directions

By hand:

1. Stir the water and vinegar together and put them in the freezer while you work.
2. In a large bowl, mix the flour, sugar and salt with your fingers or a fork.
3. Toss the butter cubes in the flour to coat them. Smash and rub the butter into the flour with your fingers until most of it is in flat pieces about ¾ inch (2 cm) across, with some smaller bits. It should still look dry and crumbly.
4. Drizzle about half of the cold water-vinegar over the bowl and toss from the bottom up, like a salad. Keep adding splashes and tossing until it looks shaggy and chunky, with no pockets of dry flour. You may not need all the liquid, so stop on the dry side.
5. Squeeze a handful. If it doesn't mostly hold together, drizzle a teaspoon more liquid over the driest spots and toss again.
6. Turn it out onto the counter and pat it into a rectangle about 1 inch (2.5 cm) thick, adding a teaspoon of liquid to any spots that still look dry.
7. Fold the dough over on itself with your hands or a bench scraper and press it back down to 1 inch thick. Repeat 2 or 3 more times, dusting with flour if it gets sticky. It should come together into a cohesive dough with streaks of butter, like marble or wood grain.
8. **Standard disks:** cut the dough in half and put each half on plastic wrap. **XL disk:** keep it in one piece.
9. Wrap tightly and press into a round or rectangle about ¾ inch (2 cm) thick. Refrigerate at least 2 hours and up to 5 days, or freeze for up to 3 months.

Food processor method:

This is faster, and it's the better choice when you're making more than one batch, the kitchen is warm, or your hands tend to melt the butter. It goes quickly, so don't get distracted and overwork the dough.

1. Freeze the cubed butter in a small bowl for 10 minutes. Mix the water and vinegar as above. Pulse the flour, sugar and salt in a food processor (at least 7-cup) 3 times to mix.
2. Add the butter (firm but not frozen solid) and pulse 3 or 4 times, 1 second each, until it's coated in flour and broken into chickpea-sized pieces, about ½ inch (13 mm).
3. Drizzle half of the water-vinegar over the mixture and pulse twice, 1 second each. Drizzle in another splash and pulse 2 or 3 more times, just until it barely comes together. It should be crumbly, with moist pebbles of dough and no dry flour. If you still see dry flour, add more liquid a tablespoon at a time with 1-second pulses. You may not need all of it.
4. Turn the dough out onto a clean counter and break up any large pieces with your fingers or a bench scraper so the crumbs are even. Continue from step 7 above. The butter pieces will be a little smaller than in the hand-mixed version, and the surface should look like terrazzo.

For a completely hands-off version, skip the folding and tip the crumbly dough straight onto plastic wrap, wrap it up, and gently roll over it with a rolling pin until it's tightly pressed together and about ¾ inch (2 cm) thick. Folding gives you flakier layers, though.

## Notes

- Use one standard disk for a regular galette, or the whole batch as one XL disk for a sheet-pan slab galette like the [Brown-Buttered Apples and Honey Galette](/recipes/brown-buttered-apples-and-honey-galette/).
- Keep everything cold, and don't stop halfway through. The 2-hour rest is the time to take a break.
- Shape the dough roughly like your galette (round or rectangle) before chilling to make rolling easier.

Rolling out the dough:

Let the chilled dough sit out for 5 to 10 minutes so it softens a little, then unwrap it, sprinkle it with flour and roll it on a lightly floured counter. Roll from the center out with firm, even pressure, flipping and turning the dough and adding flour as needed so it doesn't stick. Press any cracks back together with your fingers. If it gets sticky, put it back in the fridge for at least 10 minutes and then carry on. Your galette recipe will say which method to use.

- **Basic method** (1 standard disk, for a round or rectangular galette on a sheet pan): Roll into a round about 13½ inches (34 cm) across, or a rectangle about 14 by 11 inches (33 by 28 cm), between ⅛ and ¼ inch (3 and 6 mm) thick. Gently fold it into quarters without pressing down, move it to a parchment-lined sheet pan and unfold.
- **Slab method** (1 XL disk, for an extra-large rectangular galette on a sheet pan): Roll into a rectangle about 20 by 14 inches (50 by 36 cm), between ⅛ and ¼ inch (3 and 6 mm) thick. Fold into quarters, move it to a parchment-lined sheet pan and unfold.
- **Tiny method** (1- or 2-serving galettes on a sheet pan): Cut a standard disk into 4 pieces or an XL disk into 8 (about 95 g each) and shape each into a round disk. Roll each one into a round about 7 inches (18 cm) across and ⅛ inch (3 mm) thick. If the kitchen is warm, stack the finished rounds on a plate or sheet pan in the fridge while you roll the rest.
- **Pan method** (for a greased 9- or 10-inch/23 or 25 cm springform, cast-iron skillet, pie plate, tart pan or cake pan): Put an unlined sheet pan in the oven while it preheats. It heats the bottom of the pan and catches leaks, which matters most for springform and tart pans. Roll a standard disk into a round about 13½ inches (34 cm) across, or an XL disk to about 18 inches (46 cm), between ⅛ and ¼ inch (3 and 6 mm) thick. Fold into quarters, then drape it into the greased pan and let it slump into the corners and hang over the rim.

Baking galettes in a muffin tin:

Any fruit or vegetable galette can be baked as extra-small galettes in a standard 12-cup muffin tin, which are fun instead of cupcakes or as hors d'oeuvres for a crowd.

- Let the dough sit out for 5 minutes. Grease the muffin tin with cooking spray or neutral oil. A standard disk fills 8 cups; an XL disk makes two batches of 8.
- Cut a standard disk into 8 pieces or an XL disk into 16 (about 50 g each), shape each into a round disk, and roll each one into a round about 5½ inches (14 cm) across and ⅛ inch (3 mm) thick. If the kitchen is warm, stack the finished rounds in the fridge while you roll the rest.
- Drape each round into a cup, letting it slump into the corners and hang over the edge. Fill each with a scant ¼ cup (about 55 g) of filling and fold the edges over toward the center, pleating as you like.
- Freeze for 10 minutes, then brush the edges with egg wash and sprinkle with sugar as you would for a sweet galette.
- Bake at 375°F (190°C) until deeply golden brown, 25 to 35 minutes, rotating the tin halfway through. Cool for at least 15 minutes, then twist them out of the tin. Serve warm or at room temperature, and store as the galette recipe says.

Crust variations:

- **Warm-spice crust** (for sweet galettes, especially cranberry, apple and pear): Add ½ teaspoon each of ground cinnamon, cardamom, cloves and allspice with the flour.
- **Peppery crust** (for any savory galette, and also good with sweet apple or stone fruit): Add 2 teaspoons coarsely ground black pepper with the flour.

Dressing up galettes:

A great galette doesn't need anything else, but a crunchy topping or a dollop of something on the side makes it even better. Store-bought vanilla ice cream or whipped cream works with almost any fruit galette, and a squeeze of citrus or a spoonful of yogurt is often all a vegetable galette needs. For a bit more effort:

- **Crumble topping:** Before you make the filling, use a fork to mix ¼ cup (30 g) all-purpose flour, ¼ cup (30 g) rolled oats, 3 tablespoons (40 g) light brown sugar and ½ teaspoon Diamond Crystal or ¼ teaspoon Morton kosher salt in a medium bowl. For warmth, add ½ teaspoon each of ground cinnamon and cardamom. Gently mix in 2½ tablespoons melted unrefined coconut oil or unsalted butter until it forms large crumbs. Before you pleat the crust, sprinkle big chunks of crumble over the filling, then fold the edges over and carry on with the galette recipe. It's also great sprinkled over a single-layer cake or quick bread before baking.
- **Tangy sweet cream:** Whisk 1 cup (230 g) heavy cream, 2 tablespoons powdered sugar and a pinch of kosher salt to soft peaks. Fold in ½ cup (115 g) sour cream, whole-milk ricotta or whole-milk Greek yogurt. It should be thick but dollopable: fold in more cream a tablespoon at a time if it's too stiff, or whisk 10 to 30 seconds longer if it's too loose. Add sugar to taste. Keeps up to 24 hours in the fridge. Makes about 2½ cups (340 g).
- **Maple whip:** Whisk 1½ cups (345 g) heavy cream, 2 tablespoons maple syrup or honey, 2 teaspoons vanilla extract and a pinch of kosher salt to soft peaks. Keeps up to 24 hours in the fridge. Makes about 3 cups (380 g).
- **Nutty drizzle:** Whisk ¼ cup (65 g) tahini or unsweetened smooth nut or seed butter, 2 tablespoons water, 1 tablespoon honey or maple syrup, ¼ teaspoon each of ground cinnamon and cardamom, ¼ teaspoon almond extract (optional) and a pinch of kosher salt until smooth. Add water until it's the consistency of caramel sauce. Keeps up to 1 week in the fridge. Makes about ⅓ cup (120 g).
- **Amaro butterscotch:** In a medium saucepan over medium heat, melt ½ cup (110 g) light brown sugar and 4 tablespoons (60 g) unsalted butter, cut into small pieces, whisking occasionally, about 3 minutes. Slowly whisk in ½ cup (115 g) heavy cream, 2 tablespoons of your favorite amaro (or 1 tablespoon vanilla extract) and 1 teaspoon Diamond Crystal or ½ teaspoon Morton kosher salt. Bring to a boil, whisking constantly, and cook until it coats the back of a spoon, 6 to 8 minutes. Pour into a heatproof container and cool for 15 to 30 minutes (it thickens as it cools). Drizzle warm. Keeps up to 2 weeks in the fridge; rewarm over low heat. Makes about ¾ cup (205 g).
- **Zingy tahini:** Whisk ¼ cup (65 g) tahini, 2 tablespoons fresh lime juice or rice vinegar, 1 teaspoon honey and a big pinch of kosher salt until smooth. Add water until it's the consistency of whole-milk yogurt, and season with more salt to taste. Keeps up to 1 week in the fridge. Makes about ½ cup (100 g).
- **Garlicky yogurt:** Whisk 1 cup (230 g) whole-milk Greek yogurt or labneh, 2 tablespoons fresh lemon juice, 1 grated garlic clove, ¼ teaspoon Diamond Crystal or a pinch of Morton kosher salt and a few grinds of black pepper until smooth. Season with more salt and lemon to taste. Keeps up to 2 days in the fridge. Makes about 1 heaping cup (275 g).
